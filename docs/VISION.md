# Verdure: visual and experiential direction

## The idea

Verdure begins with a contradiction: a polished stone small enough to hold between two fingers appears to contain a landscape too large to fit inside it.

The reference is the visual language of moss agate and dendritic inclusions. Dense green mineral growth reads as canopy. Dark voids read as ravines or impossible depth. Smoky lilac-grey translucency reads as fog, distant sky or water. The brain does half the rendering, which is exactly what makes the image powerful.

The project should preserve that ambiguity rather than resolve everything into botanical realism.

## Emotional target

The desired state is quiet absorption: the feeling of being alone in a forest without the anxiety of being stranded in one.

The experience should feel:

- still, but alive;
- large, despite originating in something tiny;
- irregular and old;
- slightly uncanny without becoming ominous;
- contemplative rather than game-like.

## Signature transition

The exterior forest is deliberately compressed into a near-flat mineral inclusion. On entry, that same procedural world rotates out of the stone's plane and expands to human scale. The visual trick is part of the fiction: the inclusion does not conceal a separate level. It becomes the level.

This is the central Verdure gesture and should remain recognisable as the project evolves.

## Restraints

Avoid:

- uniform tree scatter;
- bright fantasy bioluminescence everywhere;
- conventional game HUDs;
- large settings panels;
- exaggerated bloom;
- stock photorealistic tree assets that make the scene visually generic;
- constant motion merely because the renderer can animate it.

Prefer:

- clustered density with true clearings;
- low-frequency terrain changes;
- fog that hides more than it reveals;
- limited, mineral-derived colour;
- slow movement;
- procedural geometry with visible imperfection;
- moments of darkness and visual silence.

## Palette

The palette comes from a specimen rather than a fantasy forest:

- near-black green background;
- deep moss and olive foliage;
- muted bark brown;
- smoky celadon glass;
- occasional grey-lilac inclusions;
- pale, cool daylight.

## Sound

Sound is opt-in. The first implementation synthesises a quiet filtered wind bed and a low water-like tone with the Web Audio API, so the core experience has no audio asset dependency.

Later sound should remain sparse. A distant bird every few minutes is more valuable than an endless wildlife loop.
